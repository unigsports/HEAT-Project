Design idea, not a tested product

How a fixed ultrasound array could find a hidden lamination

A green tile can look perfect on top and still hold a thin air gap inside. Sound sent up through the tile is blocked by that gap. Many fixed sender-and-receiver pairs across the belt let the moving tile scan itself. Drag the 3D view, change the number of pairs, and watch what each design can and cannot see.

Reading the demo

  • Why a fixed array: sound through air and ceramic is weak, so each point needs time to average the signal. A single pair has to sweep across a tile that is already moving past, so it draws only a few diagonal lines (switch to "One scanning pair" to see the gaps).
  • Spacing sets what you can see: a lamination narrower than the gap between measuring lines can slide between them and be missed. More pairs means a smaller gap and a higher cost.
  • Design limits shown here: the tile rests on two rails and the sensors sit between them, so about 40 mm at each edge is not measured and needs another check.
  • Not shown: dust, belt vibration, neighbouring pairs hearing each other, and electronic noise. The sound levels in this demo are simulated, not recorded.
  • Evidence status: air-coupled ultrasound has been used on green ceramic samples in laboratories and sold as a commercial scanner. We found no published example on a 600 mm green tile at line speed. This is a design to bench-test first.
